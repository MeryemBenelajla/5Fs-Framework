The background

Learning to grow with every step.

Relocating to a new country, learning a new language, or building a professional network from zero can feel daunting. These five themes are what kept me grounded across every border — and they can help you build your own path, wherever you're heading next.

Connecting scientific discovery across global boundaries — stretching from Casablanca to top hubs in Europe, a venture-building placement in Canada, VC training in London, and MBA programming in the United States.

The challenge

Building high-level skills is only half the battle.

Many talented professionals master their craft but are never taught how to navigate a shifting career market. As traditional career ladders give way to dynamic, project-driven work, knowing how to position and communicate your strengths becomes just as critical as the work itself.

The same pattern shows up at scale in deep tech, where the stakes are measured in years and funding rounds rather than job titles. Europe isn't short on breakthrough science — it's short on the leadership capacity to carry that science across the valley of death:

1,800+

breakthrough innovations have come out of EIC-funded Pathfinder and Transition projects.

~140

of those have become startups so far — with nearly 200 more planned.

30%

of EIC Accelerator companies had a female CEO, CTO or CSO in 2025.

Source: European Innovation Council, Impact Report 2026.

The science clears the bar far more often than it becomes a company. As panels at EIC Summit 2026 put it plainly: Europe doesn't have an innovation problem, it has a scaling problem — the talent, science and IP are there, what's scarce is the leadership capacity to carry them across. The same summit's discussion on gender-diverse leadership made a parallel point: diversity isn't a pipeline shortfall, it's a design choice made early or not at all. Both are really the same lesson — the gap that decides who gets through isn't talent, it's whether anyone taught you how to navigate what comes next. That's the gap the 5Fs are built to close, for anyone making the crossing.

The framework

The five elements of professional direction.

Each of these five steps is a lesson drawn from direct, real-world experience — and maps naturally onto the competencies deep-tech teams need most to reach market: moving from a technology-first mindset to human-centric value propositions, brokering the right ecosystem connections, and building the business model and strategic foresight to carry a breakthrough forward.

F1
Identity & Growth · Human-Centric Thinking

Find Yourself

When titles or locations change, who are you at your core? Real stability starts with understanding your own values and what makes your perspective unique.

F2
Core Talent & Adaptive Strengths · Business Modelling

Find Your Strength

True professional advantage isn't tied to one tool, task, or workplace. Your biggest asset is your ability to learn and master new ideas quickly.

F3
Network & True Alignment · Ecosystem Brokering

Find Your People

Arriving somewhere new without a single contact is part of the process. Building a community isn't magic — it's a skill you can practice deliberately.

F4
Authentic Expression & Influence · Empathic Leadership

Find Your Voice

In large organizations, the people who earn real trust are the ones who speak clearly and stay honest. Your natural point of view is your greatest asset in any conversation.

F5
Impact & Visionary Purpose · Strategic Foresight

Find Your Purpose

Every project, task and pivot feels different when it serves a larger goal. Purpose is the compass that gives your hardest work lasting meaning.

The journey, stage by stage

Five habits of mind that turn "stuck after years in academia" into a clear next move.

1
Stage 1 · Self-Awareness

Reframe Your Identity

Find Yourself. Separates who you are from your degree or job title, so the honest starting point is "who am I beyond my research," not just what you've published.

2
Stage 2 · Skills Translation

Translate Your Strength

Find Your Strength. Turns deep technical mastery into language and evidence that make sense outside academia — to recruiters, hiring managers and investors alike.

3
Stage 3 · Network Building

Build Your Crew

Find Your People. Deliberately builds the mentors, peers and connectors most researchers are never taught to seek out — the difference between a closed lab and an open career.

4
Stage 4 · Visibility & Voice

Find Your Voice

Find Your Voice. Builds the confidence to communicate value and ideas clearly outside technical or academic settings, where credibility isn't assumed by default.

5
Stage 5 · Direction & Purpose

Set Your Course

Find Your Purpose. Turns purpose into the compass that decides which opportunities are worth taking — and which aren't, however impressive they look on paper.

The journey, stage by stage

The "valley of death" between a grant-funded prototype and a fundable, human-centric business — a set of leadership shifts, not a one-time funding problem.

1
Stage 1 · Customer Discovery

Reframe the Offer

Find Yourself + Find Your Voice. Separates the team's identity from their invention, so the pitch starts with the customer's problem instead of the technology.

2
Stage 2 · Ecosystem Brokering

Map the Ecosystem

Find Your People. Systematic stakeholder mapping and warm introductions across investors, corporates, TTOs, regulators and pilot customers — because the gap between university-owned IP and the venture world is a structural one, not a networking failure a founder should have to solve alone.

3
Stage 3 · Business Modelling

Build the Revenue Model

Find Your Strength. Turns validated learning into a defensible, fundable business model — the exact shift that closes the valley of death, and the same one behind turning a lab result into a spin-off.

4
Stage 4 · Empathic Leadership

Hold the Team Together

Find Your Voice. Keeps technical and business co-founders aligned when strategy pivots under investor or market pressure.

5
Stage 5 · Strategic Foresight

Keep the Long View

Find Your Purpose. Scenario planning that keeps a team anchored to mission through funding cycles and shifting market conditions.
